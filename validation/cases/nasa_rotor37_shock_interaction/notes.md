# Rotor 37 shock / viscous interaction evidence

NASA TM-107310, Table 7, printed p. 195 (PDF p. 215). The image was read
twice, cell by cell; OCR was used only to locate the table. Printed ditto
marks in speed and flow columns are retained. The table headings use
Unicode subscripts where available and underscores for multi-letter
subscripts. No reported values have been corrected or converted.

This is the **same Rotor 37 family**, measured in a separate detailed-flow
investigation. It is not a held-out machine and is not added to the frozen
108-job comparison. The qualitative flow labels are not mass-flow inputs.
Do not identify these points with Stage 37 reading numbers.

The quantities M1 and M2 are local Mach numbers upstream/downstream of the
shock; M1 is not the row-inlet Mach used to normalize total-pressure loss.
Theta is observed turning; beta is the estimated shock inclination; Mn is
the corresponding normal Mach. Their mutual relation is partly used to
infer the shock angle (eq. 28), so it is not an independent validation of
the ideal oblique-shock equations.

Total loss comes from aerodynamic probes. Shock loss is estimated from the
mid-pitch laser measurements using eqs. 28-30. Profile loss is their
difference, including secondary flow and downstream mixing. It is neither
pure blade skin friction nor a directly measured separation loss. The
report allows about 0.01 bias in this decomposition due to particle lag.
The 100/high/30 and 100/max/70 printed budgets differ by 0.001; retain them.

Printed pp. 195-196 associate the design-speed cases with shock-induced
separation and the 80/85% cases with insufficient shock strength to separate
the suction boundary layer. The profile losses are not monotone in Mn.
The report attributes this to shock location, curvature and pressure
gradient. Printed p. 200 warns against extending low-speed diffusion/loss
correlations to shock-dominated loading. Neither observation supplies a
quantitative entropy/displacement-thickness closure.

Table 6, printed p. 183 (PDF p. 203), additionally preserves four integrated
blockage results. These are radial averages over the core or the identified
endwall-defect region (eq. 26), not whole-annulus area averages. For the
high-flow condition core blockage is 9% at design speed and 4% at 80/60%
speed; corresponding endwall values are 17%, 14% and 10%, over regions whose
span bounds differ. Values are printed as integer percentages. This is
direct evidence for the speed dependence of measured blockage, not a fitted
correction to Aungier's wake or endwall factors. Their definitions and survey
plane must be reconciled before a numerical comparison. The flow labels
alone do not specify a runnable operating point.

Figures containing detailed blockage distributions are not digitized here.
Neither table validates a shock-location predictor or whole-stage efficiency
response by itself.
